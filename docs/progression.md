# The ladder

How a player climbs from bare hands to iron, one page. No stone tools and
no diamond tools: **wood → bronze → iron**, climbed the way people climbed
it. V opens the Craft tab (the recipes you can make by hand); a station
shows its own when you use it with an empty hand.

## Hands

Bare hands move earth, sand, gravel, clay and snow, fell the soft logs
(an axe would be quicker, and the game says so once), and break **flint**
out of its bed. They will not move rock: "Bare hands will not move stone.
Fire will crack it, or a bronze pick will break it."

By hand: **sticks** from a log, **tinder** from dry grass or needles,
**bark strips** from a log, **cord** from bramble, a **fire striker** from
two flint, **torches** (a stick, a bark strip, a tinder), a **campfire**
(three sticks, two logs, a tinder), and a **workbench** (four logs, four
cord).

## Fire

Lay the campfire and strike it with the striker. It burns twenty minutes
and takes logs for more. After thirty seconds it **cracks the rock around
it** — stone, slate, calcite, basalt, copper and iron ore, coal — and
cracked rock comes away by hand, giving the rock whole. Dig the cracked face
back and the fire reaches the next layer. Granite and the hard rocks do not
crack.

A fire opens with an empty hand: put raw meat on it and it roasts (leave it
and it chars), put meat and fruit on it with a copper pot and it stews, put
wet clay on it and it dries.

## Wood

At the workbench: **planks** split from a log with a wedge, **hafts**,
**digging sticks** (the first tool: earth, faster), **wedges**, **mauls**
(the fastest thing on cracked rock), a **chest**, a **sluice**. Ironwood
makes each of them last twice as long, once you have an axe to fell it.

## Clay

Wet clay and cobbles at the workbench are a **kiln**; its first fire makes
it one. At **red heat** (logs, planks, sticks) it burns logs to
**charcoal**, fires clay, and fires the **crucible** and the **moulds**
pressed from wet clay at the bench. It bakes bread and roasts meat.

## Bronze

At **orange heat** (charcoal, or coal from the deeper seams), 27 units of
ore in a crucible are an **ingot**: copper from the surface, cracked out by
fire; tin from deep ore, or nine **tin grains** a **sluice** washes out of
river gravel (and every ninth wash, a flake of gold). Nine copper and one
tin are ten **bronze**. Bronze poured into a mould is a **head** — the mould
cracks after four pours — and a head and a haft at the workbench are a
**bronze pick, axe, spade, chisel, hammer, knife, sickle or hoe**.

Bronze breaks rock and every ore down to gold, and granite, slowly. It wears
quickly: the five pips beside the hotbar say how much is left, and a red bar
under the crosshair says when what you hold cannot break what you point at.

## Iron

Iron ore glows in a kiln and does nothing. It wants a **bloomery** (fired
clay, stone and a cast bronze tuyere), which burns **charcoal only** — coal's
sulphur spoils the bloom — and burns **white** with **bellows** in it (planks,
cord and a cast copper nozzle). Two parts ore to one of charcoal, two
minutes, is an **iron bloom**.

Square a block of granite with a bronze chisel and it is an **anvil**. Put
the bloom on it and strike it three times with a hammer: a **wrought iron
bar**. Bars become iron heads under an **iron** hammer — and the first iron
hammer's head is forged with the bronze one, in eight blows at twice the
wear. Hafted, iron is the first tool you keep, and the only one that breaks
obsidian, diamond, orichalcum and the other hard rocks.

## And then

Diamond, orichalcum, crystal, silver, gold and lead are mined and kept.
What they are for is the next mods' — progress, then magic or tech.
